Soil Grouping & Sustainable Rotation Advice

Policy Guide

To prevent nutrient depletion and support sustainable cultivation, agricultural policies recommend dividing regional soils into clusters and planning rotations. Select a soil type to see recommended crops:

Drought-Resilient Rotation Program

These crops survive in areas with limited water availability. Recommended rotation sequence: Legumes (mothbeans, mungbean) -> Grains (millets) -> Oilseeds to maintain soil structure and nitrogen.

Policy Action: Introduce subsidy structures for micro-irrigation systems (drip/sprinkler) in these crop zones to conserve aquifer reserves by 40%.
Wetland Crop Management

Crops requiring high humidity and persistent moisture. Rotation with deep-rooting cover crops during dry periods helps control soil erosion and maintains sub-surface drainage channels.

Policy Action: Support laser land-leveling and bund building programs. Direct efforts to build local drainage structures to avoid runoffs and flooding.
Acidic Tolerant Cultivation

These crops thrive in soils with lower pH. If pH falls below 5.0, add agricultural lime (dolomite) to unlock key macronutrients like phosphorus which get locked in acidic environments.

Policy Action: Set up community soil-testing centers and establish agricultural lime distribution centers at subsidised rates.
Alkaline Tolerant Cultivation

Crops suited for saline or high pH coastal/inland soils. Regular addition of gypsum (calcium sulfate) or elemental sulfur helps reduce pH and improves water infiltration rates.

Policy Action: Focus on gypsum distribution schemes and build sub-surface drainage systems to flush out salts during rain cycles.

Crop-Climate Key Research Insights

N
Nitrogen Dependency: Cotton, coffee, maize, and banana are high-nitrogen consumers (mean N > 80 mg/kg), whereas legumes (lentil, chickpea) fix their own nitrogen and require < 30 mg/kg.
P
Phosphorus Spikes: Apple and grapes exhibit highly specialized soil phosphorus requirements (mean P > 120 mg/kg). Regular crops require only 40-60 mg/kg.
K
Potassium Outliers: Similar to phosphorus, grapes and apple require extreme soil potassium levels (mean K > 200 mg/kg). Standard crop averages sit around 20-40 mg/kg.
R
Rainfall Constraints: Rice and coconut are water-intensive (mean Rainfall > 180mm). Muskmelon and chickpea are dryland crops showing extreme high water vulnerability if over-irrigated.
